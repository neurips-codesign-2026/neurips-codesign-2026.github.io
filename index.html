Overview

Co-design research jointly optimizes both the structural design of a system and the control policy operating it. Once rooted in embodied intelligence and robotics, algorithmic co-design has grown into a universal framework across the computational sciences.

This workshop focuses on the role large-scale AI systems and foundation models play in guiding better physical designs. By bridging deep reinforcement learning, graph neural networks, and mixed-integer non-linear programming (MINLP), we explore automated, synergistic hardware–software development — from micro-scale semiconductor architecture to macro-scale cyber-physical infrastructure.

  • Semiconductor & quantum architecture: joint chip placement (Edge-GNNs, PPO) with compiler mapping, breaking classical EDA bottlenecks.
  • Data center networks: optical switch topologies as fluid variables; GNN-augmented deep RL co-optimizing network shape and routing.
  • Macro-scale cyber-physical systems: continuous control with discrete spatial layout — wind-farm turbine layout with wake-steering, or urban traffic topology.
  • Autonomous vehicles & aerospace: sensor-placement co-design with perception pipelines; aerodynamic wing morphology with flight control.

Call for Contributions

We invite extended abstracts (up to 4 pages, excluding references) on early-stage work, position papers, or recent breakthroughs in algorithmic co-design.

Topics include co-design frameworks (DRL, multi-agent RL, constrained bilevel optimization); differentiable simulation and robotics (differentiable physics, neural cellular automata, morphology optimization); hardware–software co-optimization (logic synthesis, accelerator design, quantum compiler routing); and infrastructure and network topology (GNNs for dynamic routing, smart-grid optimization, sensor and perception co-design).

Submission details, reviewing criteria, and deadlines will be posted after workshop acceptance.
